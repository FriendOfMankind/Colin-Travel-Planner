Greece in one minute

The Acropolis
The rock in the middle of Athens, with the Parthenon on top. It's built from white marble quarried on a mountain you can see from the summit. Below it are the Agora, the old market where Socrates argued, and the tiny whitewashed lanes of Anafiotika.
Delphi
The oracle of Apollo, on a mountainside above a valley of olive trees. The ancient Greeks called it the center of the world. People came from across the Mediterranean to ask it questions, and kings went to war on its answers.
Meteora
Huge towers of sandstone rise straight out of the plain, and monks built monasteries on the tops of them. Until the last century, the only way up some of them was a rope ladder or a net hauled up by a winch.
Sounion
A temple to Poseidon on a cliff at the very tip of the coast, facing the open sea. Lord Byron carved his name into one of the columns. It's the classic Greek sunset.
May
It's warm, about 25°C (high 70s °F), with around 14 hours of daylight. That's before the summer heat. Cruise ships are in almost every day, so the big sites get done at opening.

Good to know

Crowds

Cruise ships are in port almost every day in May. Every big site gets done right at opening, and the afternoons are for wandering.

Getting around

Athens is all on foot and metro. The Delphi–Meteora loop is either a rental car or a bus plus a train. If it's a car, Colin may need an International Driving Permit; that still needs checking.

Weather

Warm days around 25°C and cooler nights. Bring sun protection; there's very little shade on the ruins.

Dress code

The Meteora monasteries need covered shoulders and knees, and long trousers for men. Pack one pair.

Time zone

Athens is 2 hours ahead of Ireland and 7 ahead of Cleveland.

Strikes

Greece has occasional short-notice transport strikes. That's why there's a spare day.

Frisco week: skiing for everyone

Cross-country skiing is walking on skis on groomed track. Beginners are gliding within an hour. The Frisco Nordic Center is at the edge of town, at about 9,100 ft, with about 30 km of groomed ski trail and 8 km of snowshoe trail. It's projected to open Nov 26.

  • Fri 25Dec

    Arrive, take it easy

    Frisco is at 9,100 ft. Drink water and skip anything hard on day one.

  • Sat 26Dec

    Lesson day at Frisco Nordic

    A private lesson, 1 hour 15 minutes, at 10:00, 12:00 or 2:00. All lessons there are private. The lesson includes a full-day trail pass, so we keep skiing afterward. Finn already skis and can skip the lesson and just ski.

    $95 per person lesson · ~$35 classic ski rental · book ahead by phone for more than one person: 970-668-2570

  • Sun 27Dec

    Snowshoe to Mayflower Gulch

    15–20 minutes up CO-91 by car. About 3–4 miles round trip on snowshoes, gaining 550–600 ft, to the old Boston Mine cabins under a big bowl. A midday outing: sunset is around 4:45. Buses don't go here, so it's a family-car day.

  • Mon 28Dec

    Ski from Frisco to Breckenridge

    The town's recreation path is groomed for skiing on Mondays, Wednesdays and Fridays: 8 miles, point to point, from the Frisco Nordic Center to the Breckenridge Recreation Center. Monday means fresh grooming. The free bus brings anyone back who doesn't want to ski the return. Lunch in Breckenridge.

    The path is free · rentals ~$35 each · a day pass is needed only where it crosses the Nordic Center trails

  • Tue 29Dec

    Free day

    Another ski day at Frisco Nordic, Breckenridge Nordic or Gold Run. A day pass at one works at all three. Or Finn goes downhill.

  • Wed 30Dec

    You two head home

    You drive the rental to the airport and fly home. Finn and I stay at the condo.

Family ski cost, roughly: the lesson day is about $130 per beginner ($95 lesson with pass + ~$35 rental) and about $70 for Finn (pass + rental). The Breckenridge day is about $35 each in rentals. Day passes are $35 for ages 13–64 and $30 for 65 and up.

Getting around without a car

Summit Stage: free

The county bus runs between Frisco, Breckenridge, Silverthorne, Keystone and Copper. It's free, seven days a week, roughly 6 AM to 1:30 AM. The county lists the trailheads it stops at, several of them right at the stop. In Breckenridge, the town's own Free Ride runs to the Gold Run Nordic Center.

Bustang: $12–13 to Denver

The state bus runs from the Frisco Transit Center to Denver Union Station: $12 on the West Line, $13 on the Pegasus van. From Union Station, the A Line train runs to the airport. Same bus west to Vail for $5.

The cheapest version: you two drive the rental to the airport on Dec 30 as planned. Finn and I take Bustang and the train to the airport at the end. Nobody pays for a car for the extra days.

    7.8 miles one way, climbing from 9,110 ft to 10,075 ft through the forest under the Tenmile Range, on snowshoes or skis. The trailhead is 0.2 miles from the Frisco bus stop, and the free bus brings us back from Breckenridge. New Year's Eve in Frisco.

    Free buses to both. Whichever one has the better grooming report that morning.

    $35 day pass, good at all three Summit centers

  • Sat 2Jan

    Mount Royal and the Masontown ruins

    Straight up from the end of Frisco's Main Street, on foot. The Forest Service rates it "most difficult": about 1,250 ft in 2 miles to the summit you can see from town, past the remains of Masontown, an old mining camp. Microspikes. Then an afternoon ski if our legs are still working.

  • Sun 3Jan

    The Wildernest bus climbs to the Lily Pad trailhead (the stop is the trailhead). We snowshoe out onto the frozen lake under Buffalo Mountain, then walk downhill to the Meadow Creek trailhead at the edge of Frisco, so the bus does the climbing. Sapphire Point overlook in the afternoon (bus stop 0.15 mi away).

    An old railroad grade above Breckenridge: 3.5 miles to Bakers Tank, a wooden water tower from the steam days, or 6.5 miles to the 11,481 ft summit and the restored Section House. Whether a bus gets us close to the winter trailhead isn't checked yet. If not, we swap in a second Peaks Trail or Nordic day.

What could go wrong, and the plan for it

  • Avalanches. Mount Royal is steep, and the mountains above the Peaks Trail and Lily Pad Lake are avalanche terrain. We check the Colorado Avalanche Information Center (avalanche.state.co.us) every morning, stay on the trail, and turn around when it says to.
  • Lake ice. We walk only on a small, high lake that has been frozen for weeks. Not on Lake Dillon.
  • Cold and short days. Single digits in the morning and sunset around 4:45. We start early, carry layers, and every day ends back at the condo.
  • The bus home. If I-70 closes on our travel day, we wait it out at the condo. The 7-night version has more slack before the flight.

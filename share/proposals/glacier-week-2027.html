Know this

  • Grizzly country. Bear spray on your belt on every hike. Food and anything scented, toothpaste too, goes in the bear box at night.
  • Two 11-mile days. If 10 miles with some climbing sounds fine now, you're good. If not, start walking.
  • The Highline ledge is exposed. Not climbing, kids do it, but if heights get to you, turning back at the cable is the plan.
  • Early starts and cold mornings. The big hikes start 5–6 AM. Nights around 46°F, days in the mid-70s.

Fishing Swiftcurrent

Thu · the rules

Thursday afternoon, after Iceberg Lake: shore fishing at Swiftcurrent Lake, a few minutes from camp. The goal is one trout for dinner. The fish here run small, so treat it as a bonus.

  • Ethan brings the rod and tackle: a spinning rod that breaks down, in a checked bag, plus line, small spinners and hooks.
  • No license needed inside the park.
  • No lead of any kind. Steel, tin or tungsten tackle only.
  • Worms are legal here, which is rare in the park. Grab some in Kalispell if a shop has them.
  • Keep: brook trout (red spots with blue rings) and rainbow trout. Release: everything else, including any cutthroat (red slash under the jaw). Not sure? Let it go.
  • A kept fish: guts in a garbage can, skin stays on, everything fishy in the bear box.

From Glacier's fishing rules (nps.gov, 2026). Ethan: check the 2027 version before buying tackle.

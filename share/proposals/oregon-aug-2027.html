Oregon in one minute

The Gorge
The Columbia River cuts straight through the Cascade Mountains here, leaving an 80-mile canyon of basalt cliffs. The Ice Age floods that carved it left waterfalls pouring off nearly every side canyon.
Two Oregons
The mountains split the state in two. West of them it's wet and green; an hour east it turns to dry high desert, sagebrush and rimrock canyons. This trip crosses that line twice.
The volcanoes
The Cascades are a chain of volcanoes. Mt. Hood watches over the Gorge, and the Three Sisters stand west of Bend. South Sister is the tallest of the three, and the one we climb.
Lava country
Around McKenzie Pass, lava flows covered the land in black rock. One flow dammed a river and drowned a forest about 3,000 years ago. That's Clear Lake, and the dead trees are still standing underwater.
Glacier water
The White Salmon comes off the glaciers of Mt. Adams. It stays ice-cold all summer, which is exactly what you want after a hot day.
Hot springs
Volcanic heat means natural hot springs. We sleep at one and soak at another.

White Salmon: the waterfall drop

Class III–IV whitewater · a 10–12 ft waterfall in a raft · ice-cold swims · Mt. Hood at golden hour

  • RaftWhite Salmon River, a half-day of nearly nonstop Class III–IV rapids through a narrow canyon.
  • OptionalIt ends at Husum Falls, a 10–12 ft vertical drop and the tallest commercially rafted waterfall in the US. You can ride it over or walk around and watch. Guides only run it at safe water levels, and you have to be 16+.
  • Lost Lake at golden hour: a mountain lake with Mt. Hood standing right over the water.
Sleep: Sunset Motel, Hood River (night 2 of 3)
Tue Aug 3 · the big Gorge hike

Behind Tunnel Falls

Cliffside trail with cables · a bridge 150 ft up · a tunnel blasted behind a 175-ft waterfall

  • Eagle Creek to Tunnel Falls: about 12 miles round trip and 1,200 ft of climbing, on a trail cut into the canyon wall with cable handholds. It crosses High Bridge, 150 ft above the creek, and the turnaround is a tunnel hand-blasted behind 175-ft Tunnel Falls.
  • ShorterPunch Bowl Falls is 2 miles in. Anyone can stop there for a swim and wait.
  • Heads upThis canyon closes after storms some years. If it's shut in 2027, we swap in another Gorge waterfall hike.
Sleep: Sunset Motel, Hood River (night 3 of 3)
Wed Aug 4 · Maupin, then on to Bend

Desert whitewater

Basalt canyon · Class III–IV · natural rock slides · 12-ft jump at Pirate's Cove · wool-boom ghost town

  • RaftLower Deschutes, full day out of Maupin: 13 miles through a desert canyon with Class III–IV rapids (Boxcar, Oak Springs), a riverside lunch, swim stops and natural rock slides.
  • OptionalPirate's Cove: a 12-ft jump into a calm, deep cove.
  • Shaniko on the way south: a wool-boom town from 1900, now mostly empty.
Sleep: Holiday Motel, Bend (night 1 of 4)
Thu Aug 5 · dawn start

South Sister

10,358 ft · no ropes · glacier lake · crater rim · half of Oregon's volcanoes in one view

  • The summit of South Sister, Oregon's third-highest peak: about 12.5 miles round trip and 5,000 ft of climbing. It's a trail the whole way, with no ropes or technical gear, but it's long and steep, with loose red cinder near the top.
  • Partway up is Lewis Glacier's turquoise lake. From the rim you look down into a snow-filled crater and out to the other Sisters, Mt. Jefferson and Mt. Hood.
  • Heads upWeather decides it. We set a turnaround time, and thunder means down. Anyone can stop at the lake and wait.
Sleep: Holiday Motel, Bend (night 2 of 4)

Where we stay

Three bases, nine nights, one room for the four of us at each: two to a queen bed. These are Colin's picks. Nothing is booked yet; rooms get booked on refundable rates once everyone's in.

Aug 1–4 · 3 nights · Hood River

Sunset Motel

A small, recently redone motel close to downtown Hood River, with a grocery store across the street. Book the Standard Room, 2 Queen Beds, which is listed as sleeping 4, with a fridge and microwave. Guests rate it about 8.8 out of 10 across 1,000+ reviews.

~$164–226 a night for the room in summer, about $125–170 each for 3 nights.

Aug 4–8 · 4 nights · Bend

Holiday Motel

A cheap, no-frills motel about a mile from the Old Mill District and the river float. Book the Double Queen room (2 queens, for 2–4 people), with a fridge and microwave. Reviews call it clean and friendly but dated, with thin walls. We're there to sleep, not to hang out. If we end up five, its Family Suite has three queens.

~$128–160 a night for the room in summer, about $130–160 each for 4 nights.

Aug 8–10 · 2 nights · McKenzie River

Belknap Hot Springs Lodge

A rustic lodge on the McKenzie River with two hot-spring pools. The upper pool is for overnight guests only, open 9 AM–9 PM, and lodge rooms get continental breakfast and pool towels. Book a 4-guest lodge room. There are only 18 rooms in the lodge and just a few take four people, so this is the first thing we book.

$140–215 a night plus 12% tax, about $80–120 each for 2 nights.

Good to know

Smoke

Wildfire smoke hits Oregon in late summer some years. It's the one thing that can really mess with this trip. We book refundable rooms where we can, and Saturday is a spare day for moving things around.

Getting around

Everyone rides in Colin's car, so no rental. It seats five, so four people plus bags fits; five doesn't. Pack a soft duffel or backpack, not a hard suitcase.

Weather

August is hot and dry in Bend and warm in the Gorge, with cool nights in the mountains. The South Sister summit is cold and windy even in summer. Bring a warm layer and a wind jacket for that day.

The water

The rivers and lakes are cold, the White Salmon especially. You need to be able to swim for the rafting and the jumps. Guides give the safety talk; the jumps are your call.

Hot springs

Belknap requires swimsuits. Terwilliger is clothing-optional, so expect some of that.

Phones

Signal is fine in the towns and patchy in the mountains and canyons. Download offline maps before you come.

Time zone

Pacific time, 3 hours behind Cleveland. You land in the afternoon feeling like it's dinner.

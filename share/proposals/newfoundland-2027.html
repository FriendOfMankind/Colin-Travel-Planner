Newfoundland in one minute

The place
A big rocky island off Canada's east coast, closer to Ireland than to Vancouver. It was its own country until it joined Canada in 1949, and it still has its own time zone, accent and slang.
Outports
Small fishing villages on the coast, many reachable only by boat until recently. Brightly painted wooden houses, fishing sheds on stilts over the water.
Cod
For 500 years the island lived on cod. The fishery collapsed and was closed in 1992, the largest layoff in Canadian history. Cod still runs the menu: fish and chips, fish cakes, cod tongues.
Resettlement
From the 1950s to the 1970s the government paid isolated villages to move to bigger towns. The empty ones are still out there; two are on this trip.
Iceberg Alley
Every spring, icebergs that broke off Greenland's glaciers drift south past the island's northeast coast. May and June are the season.
Puffins and whales
Atlantic puffins (the provincial bird) nest on the coast all summer. In June, small fish called capelin swarm the shore, and humpback whales follow them in to eat.

Day by day

Every hike is optional. Most are 1–6 miles and easy to moderate; the one longer option (7.7 miles) is marked. Sunset times are calculated for those dates. Boat departures and opening hours get confirmed closer to June.

Gander, then Fogo Island

Come From Away town · island ferry · "edge of the flat earth" · sunset climb · oceanfront cottage

  • Gander is the town from the musical Come From Away: on 9/11, 38 airliners were diverted here and a town of about 10,000 took in their passengers for days. Colin meets you at arrivals.
  • The Farewell ferry is the only way onto Fogo with a car, and it can't be reserved, so we line up early for the 6:00 PM boat (8:30 as backup). The 45-minute crossing of Hamilton Sound is a good place to spot icebergs and whales from the deck.
  • Fogo Island is the largest of Newfoundland's offshore islands, about 25 km long, with eleven old outports. It's known for a famous design hotel and modern artist studios perched on the rocks, in a place that still feels very remote.
  • Brimstone Head: a short, steep climb with stairs up a rock headland over the town of Fogo. The Flat Earth Society calls it one of the four corners of the earth. Sunset from the top at 9:12 PM.
Sleep: Escape by the Sea, a 2-bedroom cottage with a kitchen and fire pit, on the water in Seldom, Fogo Island
Fri Jun 11 · morning ferry · ~1 h drive

Twillingate: icebergs

12,000-year-old Greenland ice · zodiac boat · humpbacks · fish cakes · lighthouse cliffs at sunset

  • Twillingate calls itself the iceberg capital of the world and has the best record on the island for icebergs grounding close to shore.
  • BoatIceberg and whale tour. Boats head out to whatever bergs are grounded nearby; the zodiacs get closer to the rocks than the big boats. Icebergs vary a lot by year: almost none in 2024, plenty in early June 2025.
  • D&T Seafood for fish cakes: salt cod mashed with potato and fried.
  • Long Point Lighthouse: a 3-mile trail along cliffs at the tip of the island, the classic spot to watch for bergs and whales. Sunset 9:15 PM.
Sleep: Twillingate (hotel)
Sat Jun 12 · ~4 h 45 m driving, with stops

Bonavista: puffins

Collapsed sea cave · Cabot's landfall · fish and chips with a view · puffins at arm's length

  • The longest drive of the week, south to the Bonavista Peninsula, where John Cabot is said to have landed in 1497.
  • Dungeon Provincial Park: a sea cave whose roof fell in, leaving a huge hole in the meadow with the ocean surging through arches at the bottom. Then the red-and-white Cape Bonavista lighthouse.
  • Little Dairy King: fish and chips, then soft-serve.
  • Elliston puffin site: a short walk to a cliff facing a rock stack covered in nesting puffins, a few dozen metres across a gap. One of the closest views of puffins from land in North America, no boat needed. If the birds are out at sea, there's a dawn retry later in the week.
Sleep: Trinity area (night 1 of 3)
Sun Jun 13

Trinity: ruins and sea stacks

Bomber wreck or ghost village · 1700s merchant town · sea stacks at golden hour

  • Trinity is one of the oldest towns on the island: a harbour of preserved 1700s and 1800s merchant buildings, used as a film set more than once.
  • Pick oneThe B-36 crash site (1.3 miles): wreckage of a US Air Force bomber that hit a hill here in 1953, killing all 23 aboard. It's a memorial, so treat it as one. Or British Harbour (7.7 miles, the long option): a resettled village from the 1960s, now foundations, a graveyard and a few cabins on a quiet cove. Or a slow morning.
  • Skerwink Trail (3.2 miles): a loop over cliffs and past sea stacks, often ranked among the best coastal walks in North America. Whales feed below it in capelin season. Sunset 9:05 PM.
Sleep: Trinity area (night 2 of 3)

Tue Jun 15 · ~3 h 20 m drive

St. John's

Provincial capital · famous fish and chips · first transatlantic radio signal · cliff ledge with chains · sunset tower

  • St. John's: the capital, one of the oldest European-settled cities in North America, built on hills around a harbour sealed by a narrow gap in the cliffs called the Narrows.
  • Ches's: the city's best-known fish and chips, since the 1950s.
  • Signal Hill: where Marconi received the first transatlantic radio signal in 1901. Cabot Tower on top, and the North Head trail (2.2 miles) down along the cliffs above the Narrows. Sunset 9:00 PM.
  • Heads upPart of North Head is a ledge about 3 feet wide along the cliff, with chains to hold. We do it first, in daylight, and skip it in wind or rain. Anyone can skip it and meet at the tower.
Sleep: St. John's (night 1 of 4)
Wed Jun 16

Cape Spear and Petty Harbour

Easternmost point in North America · first sunrise on the continent · WWII gun batteries · cod tongues · George Street

  • OptionalSunrise at Cape Spear, the easternmost point of North America, so nobody on the continent sees the sun before you. First light 4:22, sunrise 5:03 AM. The cape is just as good later in the morning.
  • Cape Spear also has the province's oldest surviving lighthouse and WWII gun emplacements that guarded the harbour. The coastal path runs up to 5.9 miles out and back; turn around whenever.
  • Petty Harbour, a working fishing village 15 minutes away: lunch at Chafe's Landing. Try cod tongues, the small fleshy piece from a cod's throat, fried. A Newfoundland classic.
  • George Street at night: two blocks of nothing but pubs and live music, including traditional Irish-Newfoundland bands.
Sleep: St. John's (night 2 of 4)
Thu Jun 17 · full moon

The city on foot, and the screech-in

Fishing village inside the city · Jellybean Row · rum shot and a cod to kiss · full moon over the Atlantic

  • Quidi Vidi: a tiny fishing village tucked inside the city limits, then the harbourfront, Water and Duckworth Streets, and Jellybean Row, the city's rows of houses painted every colour.
  • OptionalThe screech-in: the ritual for making visitors honorary Newfoundlanders. A shot of Screech rum, a few lines of local slang to repeat, and you kiss a frozen cod. Christian's Pub on George Street is the famous one; Spirit of Newfoundland runs a bookable one at 3 PM (about $25 CAD + tax).
  • Evening: the full moon rising out of the Atlantic, from Signal Hill.
Sleep: St. John's (night 3 of 4)
Fri Jun 18 · ~45 min drive each way

Puffins and whales by boat

Half a million puffins · humpbacks up close · ghost village · suspension bridge over a gorge · moonlit drive

  • BoatWitless Bay Ecological Reserve, from Bay Bulls: four islands holding North America's largest Atlantic puffin colony, around half a million birds, plus humpback and minke whales feeding on capelin, and sometimes an iceberg. About $95 CAD.
  • La Manche: the stone foundations of a fishing village destroyed by a winter storm in 1966, reached on the East Coast Trail. A suspension bridge hangs across the gorge where the village stood (1.8 miles out and back).
  • Drive back under a full moon. Last night together.

Good to know

Passport

Flying into Canada needs a valid passport book. If you don't have one, apply now; processing takes weeks to months.

Getting around

Everyone rides in Colin's Toyota Sienna minivan, so no rental car. The middle seats come out for this trip: the three of you share the back bench (three real seatbelts), and the luggage goes in the middle. Pack a soft duffel or backpack, not a big hard suitcase. Most drives are under 2 hours; the longest is about 4 h 45 m on June 12.

Weather

Cool and changeable: daytime 50s–60s °F, nights in the 30s–40s, often foggy or drizzly. Bring layers, a real rain jacket, a warm hat and shoes you can walk trails in.

Long days

Mid-June up here means sunrise around 5 and sunset after 9. Plenty of light for an evening hike after dinner.

Phones

Check whether your plan covers Canada. Signal is patchy outside towns, so download offline maps before you go.

Bugs

Black flies can be bad in the woods on still evenings in June. On the windy coast they're usually fine. Bring repellent; a head net is cheap insurance.

Time zone

Newfoundland is 1½ hours ahead of Cleveland. Yes, a half hour.

For one or two of you, on top of the week. While you're along, gas, campsites, ferries and groceries are split evenly; your flights are yours. Tell Colin by January 31. If you've already booked flights, an add-on means changing that ticket.

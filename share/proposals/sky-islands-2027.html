Southeast Arizona · spring break 2027

Sky Islands

Eight nights camping across three mountain ranges that rise out of the desert like islands. Forests of balanced rock, 1.4-billion-year-old granite domes, a canyon full of birds people fly in from other continents to see. Want in?

Sat Mar 6 → Sun Mar 14 3 people · 1 SUV 8 nights · ~32 mi hiking Fly CLE ⇄ TUS

The plan, day by day

  1. Sat 6Day 1

    Land in Tucson

    Pick up the SUV, stock up on groceries and water, pitch at Gilbert Ray among the saguaros. Short walk to the Signal Hill petroglyphs at sunset.

  2. Sun 7Day 2

    Cochise Stronghold no water

    East into the Dragoon Mountains, where Cochise held off the US Army for a decade. Granite domes, a steep short climb under Rockfellow Dome.

    2.0 mi · 1,108 ft
  3. Mon 8Day 3

    Slavin's Mine, then the hoodoos

    An abandoned mine at the head of a canyon almost nobody walks. Then into Chiricahua National Monument for three nights, and Sugarloaf to see everything we'll hike.

    6.9 mi · 1,407 ft  +  1.9 mi · 492 ft
  4. Tue 9Day 4

    Echo Canyon at first light

    The grottoes, where rock columns close overhead. Trailhead at 6:30 before the tiny lot fills, then Massai Point at 6,870 ft. Afternoon off.

    3.4 mi · 561 ft
  5. Wed 10Day 5

    Heart of Rocks

    The reason the monument exists: a loop through a forest of balanced rhyolite. Big Balanced Rock, Punch and Judy, Duck on a Rock. Starts at camp.

    6.9 mi · 1,597 ft · the big day
  6. Thu 11Day 6

    Fort Bowie, then the long way around

    Adobe ruins of the fort at Apache Pass, then 2½ hours around the range to Cave Creek, because the 8-mile road over the top is closed in winter.

    4.3 mi · 597 ft
  7. Fri 12Day 7

    Cave Creek, your pick no water

    A: the famous birding canyon, an easy creek walk under sycamores. B: Herb Martyr, a hard climb up the canyon wall. We vote.

    A: 6.3 mi · ~600 ft  /  B: 5.9 mi · 2,083 ft
  8. Sat 13Day 8

    Bisbee and Tombstone

    Back toward Tucson through Bisbee, a town stacked up a canyon on stairs next to an open copper pit. Lunch there, Boothill in Tombstone, last night at Gilbert Ray.

  9. Sun 14Day 9

    Fly home

    Back Sunday so nobody misses Monday.

What to know going in

  • Three nights have no water at camp. We haul it in jugs, roughly a gallon each per day.
  • Early starts on the big days. The best trailheads have tiny lots in their busiest month.
  • Long stretches without cell service: the Dragoons, Apache Pass, Cave Creek. Download offline maps before you fly.
  • Fuel canisters can't fly, so stove fuel gets bought in Tucson.
  • Arizona skips daylight saving. It starts the day we fly home, so check your flight time in Arizona time.

Bring

  • Tent (or we figure out sharing), and a sleeping bag good to around freezing
  • Pad, headlamp, puffy and a warm hat for 6 AM starts
  • Sun hat, sunglasses, sunscreen. The rock glare up high is no joke.
  • Two big water bottles and some electrolytes
  • Hiking boots you trust on loose rock

The two halves in one minute

Dingle
A harbour town at the end of a peninsula in County Kerry, as far west as Ireland goes. Around Slea Head there are stone beehive huts, an Iron Age fort on a cliff edge and a 1,200-year-old church built without mortar.
Marseille
France's oldest city, founded by Greek sailors 2,600 years ago. It's a working port, not a postcard, and that's the appeal: the old quarter of Le Panier, the fishing harbour under the Corniche, and the basilica on the hill that watches over all of it.
The Calanques
Narrow inlets cut into white limestone cliffs, with clear water at the bottom. Mid-May is the best hiking of the year: warm, before the summer fire closures and the August crowds.
May
Kerry is mild and changeable, so bring a rain jacket. Marseille is warm and sunny, with long evenings. Whit Monday (May 17) is a French holiday, so buses run a reduced schedule that day.

Slea Head

Local bus to the end of Ireland · Dunquin Pier · the Blasket Centre · Gallarus Oratory

  • No car needed: the 9 AM local bus gets us to Dunquin in half an hour. The zig-zag path down the cliffs to the old Blasket ferry pier, the Blasket Centre, then hop buses back past early Christian sites (Riasc, Kilmalkedar) and the 1,200-year-old Gallarus Oratory.
  • Buses are a few a day, so we plan around them. The cliffs are unfenced; nobody goes near the edge in wind.

Eask Tower · a WWII lookout · fish and chips on the harbour

  • A steep walk out of town to Eask Tower, an 1847 famine-era stone marker 600 feet above the bay, with a wartime lookout post on the same hill. Then the harbour and an easy afternoon.

  • The Port-Miou → Port-Pin → En-Vau loop, about 7 miles. En-Vau is the famous one: a narrow slot between sheer white walls, with a beach at the end.

Good to know

No car

Ireland is coaches and local buses. Marseille is metro, city buses and the odd train. Pack light enough to carry through two coaches and a city on day two.

Hiking

Three Calanques days, each 3–7 miles on rocky limestone trail. Every one has a shorter version. Bring real shoes, a hat and plenty of water.

Swimming

Possible at Sugiton and En-Vau, not promised. The sea in mid-May is fresh.

Eating in Marseille

Breakfast in the apartment, packed lunches on hike days, and wood-fired pizza (the city's everyday food) most nights. On weekdays, the set lunch (formule, about €20–26) is the best-value meal going, but it isn't served on weekends or holidays. Dinner starts around 8. Tap water is free if you ask for une carafe d'eau, and service is included in the bill.

Watch out at Saint-Charles

Marseille's main station has a known scam: someone asks for change, then leads you "to a pharmacy" down a side street. Don't follow anyone off the forecourt, and keep a hand on bags there.

Time zones

Ireland is 5 hours ahead of Cleveland, and France is 6.

Treats to find

Panisses (chickpea fries) and sugared chickpea doughnuts at Chez Magali in L'Estaque, a few euros each, and navettes, Marseille's orange-blossom biscuits, from a bakery that's been making them for about 200 years.

# Point Reyes + Marin

*The San Andreas, tule elk and redwoods*

## Why

🔻 **No longer a Maui bookend** *(decided 2026-10-05)*: Newfoundland leaves May 23, three days after Maui ends, so Maui flies straight home. This stands alone now, for a PTO year. *(was: subtitle "The Maui return leg")*

The San Andreas runs straight through it — the Earthquake Trail has a fence line **offset about 20 feet by the 1906 rupture**, and the whole peninsula is a slab of granite rafted roughly 300 miles north from the southern Sierra. Tule elk at Tomales Point (~9.4 mi, nearly flat), Bear Valley out to Arch Rock, Chimney Rock. **Maximally unlike Maui** — cold fog, dairy ranches and a transform fault, three days after a shield volcano.

⚠️ **NPS is explicit: there is no car camping inside the Seashore.** All four in-park campgrounds are hike-in. The base is **Samuel P. Taylor SP** — 60 developed plus 16 tent-only sites in redwoods on Lagunitas Creek, reservable through ReserveCalifornia, about 35 min from Bear Valley. Treat it as a redwood basecamp with coastal day trips rather than a compromise.

⚠️ **May fog and wind are the real risk** — the outer point is one of the windiest, foggiest places on the Pacific coast. Keep Chimney Rock and the lighthouse flexible; Bear Valley, Olema Valley and Samuel P. Taylor stay clear when the point is socked in. ✅ Elk: fence removal began Dec 2024 but most of the herd still holds the Tomales Point reserve, so the viewing stands.

Food: **Point Reyes Station** — Cowgirl Creamery, Bovine Bakery, and Tomales Bay oysters as the regional dish.

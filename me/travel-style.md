# Travel style

From the traveller interview on 2026-09-28 unless marked.

## What makes a trip great

1. **Big views and scenery**
2. **Exploring and discovery:** ruins, odd places, finding things that weren't on the list. This matches the Blevins-farm night (`trips/kentucky-2026/log.md`).
3. **Solitude:** having it to himself. Consistent with "put the hard, empty hike on the busiest day" (`me/hiking.md`).

*(stated 2026-09-28)*

**Physical challenge was not picked**, yet Honey Creek (ropes, ladders) was the best part of Kentucky. *Hypothesis:* the challenge is the price of reaching somewhere empty and scenic, not the point in itself. Unconfirmed; ask before building a trip around difficulty.

## Where trips go wrong

- **Too much planned.** Days get overstuffed and rushed. *(stated 2026-09-28)*
- **Food falls apart.** The planned meals don't happen. *(stated 2026-09-28; confirmed: kentucky-2026 log, where the recipe plan collapsed into staples. Answer: `kitchen/staples.md`)*

## How detailed a plan should be

- **Hour by hour.** He likes knowing. *(stated 2026-09-28)*
- The tension with "too much planned" is the planning rule: **detailed, but with slack that stays empty.** Every day needs a real slack line and a named first cut, and a day whose scheduled blocks fill it end to end is overloaded even if each block is realistic. The site should make each day's load visible.

## Driving

- **Transfer or getting-there days: 8 h+ is fine** (Colorado and Vegas road trips). *(stated 2026-09-28)*
- **Non-transfer days: 1–2 h** to trailheads (`me/hiking.md`).

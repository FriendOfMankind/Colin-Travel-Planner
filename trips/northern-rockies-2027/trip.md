# The Long Way to Glacier

> Solo out on I-80 (Vedauwoo and the Snowy Range, then the Bighorns and the Beartooth Plateau), then **Yellowstone: Lamar Valley at dusk and dawn, and the icons, the Grand Canyon of the Yellowstone, Old Faithful and Grand Prismatic**, then **seven nights at Glacier**, then west to the Columbia Gorge for **the Oregon boys week, Aug 1–10**, and home from Portland. July, because every anchor is out of season in June. ✅ Glacier has no vehicle reservation (2026 rules; 2027 not announced). 🚨 **The risk is length: 33 nights, three times your longest trip.** Days 6, 18, 20 and 27 are soft on purpose.

## Overview

Out on I-80 to southern Wyoming (Vedauwoo, then Medicine Bow Peak in the Snowy Range), north across Wyoming and Montana to Glacier, then **west to the Columbia River Gorge**, where the friends fly into Portland for **the Oregon boys week, Aug 1–10** (Gorge → Bend → McKenzie River). Then the long drive home from Portland. Five anchors on the way out (the Snowy Range, the Cloud Peak Skyway, the Beartooth Plateau, two nights in Yellowstone, **seven nights at Glacier**), then the friends' week.

### Dates

July 12 – August 14, 2027 · 34 days / 33 nights *(Colin, 2026-10-29: leave a day earlier to make room for a second Yellowstone day. The 2026-10-10 week-later shift still holds, so the Grinnell Glacier trail is open: it opened ~July 15 in 2026)*

### Group

**Solo Jul 12 – Jul 31.** **Aug 1–10: Colin + a group of friends** who fly CLE ⇄ PDX *(stated 2026-10-15)*. Headcount not recorded, and it decides the car: Colin + 3 fits the Legacy, 5 means a rental minivan. **Solo again Aug 10–14** for the drive home.

### Why July, not June

June is the gap month on this whole list: US-212 opened May 23 2026 and was closed again by snow until June 9, Snowy Range's campgrounds open July 4, and the San Juans hold snow into July. Moving three weeks fixed more than any destination swap.

### Why Glacier anchors it

The best hikes per day on the list, and ✅ **no timed-entry vehicle reservation** in 2026, the first year in five. What's left: a 3-hour parking limit at Logan Pass and a $1 ticketed shuttle (both 2026 pilots), and the campground race. 2027's rules aren't announced *(checked 2026-10-29)*.

### The booking date

**Mid-January 2027.** Glacier camping is a 6-month rolling window released daily, and the good campgrounds go the day they open. Slough Creek is worse: **16 sites, gone in minutes.** Yellowstone's other campgrounds, Madison included, move to the same 6-month recreation.gov window from 2027 (📋 nps.gov/yell, snippet, 2026-10-29). The briefing computes each date.

### Length

The profile says 2–5 campgrounds and 5–10 nights. This is **33 nights**, three times the 11-day Appalachians run. **Days 6, 18, 20 and 27 are deliberately soft**, and that's the mitigation. The lever if it's too much: a friend rides home and splits the driving.

### Timing vs Newfoundland

**Newfoundland** (`trips/newfoundland-2027/`) gets home ~June 24, and this leaves July 12: eighteen days to rest and repack. Newfoundland goes in the Sienna, so the Legacy starts this trip fresh.

- **Glacier, late July (Days 12–18):** in 2026 Montana DEQ had an air quality alert covering Glacier County on Jul 27, and on Jul 31 smoke from the Skillet Fire was forecast over the northern half of the park. - **Oregon (Aug 1–10):** Bend averaged **7.8 days a year at "unhealthy for sensitive groups" or worse, 2013–2025**, against 0.5 a year before 2013. The McKenzie valley (Belknap) was spared in 2026.
- **What it means:** one smoky summer doesn't make a pattern, but a 34-day trip through late July and early August should expect *some* smoke days. The plan already has the levers: soft days, refundable motels, and the 4-day window at Glacier. The open question below asks how to use them. The real point is the **spread**: this trip runs from a 92°F shadeless badland to a 34°F night at 9,518 ft and back, twice, and after the front-end cut it now does the first half of that swing **in a single day** (Day 4). **The sleep system is correctly specified for the Beartooth camp and massively over-specified for everything else**, which is the right way round. Pack for the range, not either end.

## Packing

### The range problem — this trip's defining one

- [ ] **Full cold kit AND full heat kit, simultaneously, for 34 days.** Beartooth at 34°F and the Deschutes canyon in August are in the same duffel.
- [ ] Siesta 20 + MondoKing — right for Beartooth, overkill everywhere else. Take both anyway.
- [ ] Sun hoody and a wide-brim hat for the Oregon desert days
- [ ] Rain shell that actually works — Bighorn and Beartooth afternoon storms are near-daily in July

### Altitude — stepped

- [ ] **Three steps up: Vedauwoo 8,300 ft (Day 2), Brooklyn Lake 10,500 ft (Day 3), West Tensleep 9,100 ft (Day 4).** The big jump is the first night, from ~1,000 ft. Take the Lakes Trail on Day 3 if that night went badly.
- [ ] Electrolytes and more water than feels necessary for the first two Bighorn days

### Bear country — new on this trip

- [ ] **Bear spray, on the person, not in the car.** Required thinking from Lamar Valley on Day 10 through the last Glacier morning on Day 19.
- [ ] Discipline about the bear box: all food, cookware, toiletries, anything scented, every single night
- [ ] Noise on blind corners. Iceberg Lake, Grinnell and the Swiftcurrent valley are all documented corridors.

## Open questions

### Can you actually get Slough Creek, Madison, Many Glacier and a west-side campground for these dates?

**Blocks:** Days 10–11 and 12–18 — over a third of the trip

Slough Creek is 16 sites and gone in minutes; Madison moves to recreation.gov for 2027, so how fast it goes is unknown; Many Glacier and Apgar go the day their windows open. **All four windows fall in mid-to-late January 2027 — book them in one sitting, with fallbacks already written.** If Many Glacier fails, Two Medicine and St. Mary reshape the middle. **Blocks:** Day 10, and how bad the Slough Creek race actually is

**Blocks:** Day 3, Medicine Bow Peak

## Notes

### The 21-day cut: what it bought and what it cost

This started as a 25-day draft and the four days came off the **front end**: Custer State Park and Devils Tower are gone, and Badlands now runs straight into the Bighorns.

That was chosen over cutting Theodore Roosevelt or a Glacier night, and it is the better trade — **it added a seventh Glacier night rather than costing one.** Seven nights is what makes the Glacier block work: an easy Saturday, Iceberg on Sunday, Grinnell on a Monday, the transfer on Tuesday, the Highline Trail on a Wednesday, and a whole day held in reserve on Thursday. The two hardest hikes land on the two best days of the week available.

**Three real costs, none hidden:**
• **Black Elk Peak is gone** — the best hike on the eastern half, and it was nearly free since I-90 passes it either way. Devils Tower goes with it.
• **The altitude step is gone.** The Black Hills at 6,200 ft used to break the climb from the plains to the Bighorns in two. *(Restored 2026-10-30 by the Snowy Range swap: Vedauwoo 8,300 ft, then Brooklyn Lake 10,500 ft, then the Bighorns. See log.)*
• **Sixteen days to the first shower.** Custer State Park was the only mid-route one. Apgar on Day 16 is now it. Cheap to solve with a paid shower in Cody or Great Falls; easy to forget until day nine.

### Three hikes are over the ceiling, on purpose, with bail-outs

The soft ceiling is ~10 mi and ~2,500 ft per day, exceeded "when the payoff justifies it, not by accident." Three scheduled hikes cross it and all three are over on distance only:

**Lost Twin Lakes (Day 5): 11.1 mi, 2,076 ft.** A mile over, at 9,000+ ft, on your second day at altitude after the biggest single-day climb of the trip. Bail-out written into the day: Mirror Lake alone at 5.9 mi / 1,095 ft, which is a complete day.

**Grinnell Glacier (Day 15): 11.1 mi, 2,047 ft.** A mile over, on day fifteen. Bail-out: the boat shuttle across Swiftcurrent and Josephine removes about 3.4 miles, and it exists precisely for this.

**The Highline to The Loop (Day 17): 11.8 mi one way, +2,388 / −4,773 ft.** Chosen by Colin *(Colin, 2026-10-29)*. Under the gain ceiling going up; the cost is the 2,300 ft descent from the chalet. Bail-outs: turn around at the cable for Hidden Lake, or the Haystack Butte out-and-back if the shuttle fails.

Four more are named in the Hikes tab and deliberately **not** scheduled: the full Highline Trail (15.2 mi / 2,572 ft — over on both counts, hence the one-way version), Haystack Butte (the out-and-back the one-way version replaced), Ptarmigan Tunnel (10.7 mi, now the Day 18 slack option rather than a scheduled day), and Cloud Peak (22.6 mi / 5,337 ft, a backpacking trip and therefore excluded by a locked rule). **Listing what was cut is the point — otherwise the ceiling gets crossed by accident rather than by decision.**

### The Garden Wall ledge, stated plainly

The first mile of the Highline Trail is cut into the side of the Garden Wall with a hand cable bolted to the rock and a long drop below it. It is **not** technical, it is **not** a scramble, and thousands of people walk it every summer including children.

It is also the single highest-consequence piece of ground on this entire route, and the profile is explicit: sustained low-consequence difficulty is welcome, single high-consequence moves get more caution. **So: if the ledge does not feel right, turn around at the cable.** Hidden Lake Overlook from the same parking lot is a genuinely good day and costs nothing but the ego. The page's job here is to give the real numbers and a stated fallback, not to nag and not to stay silent.

### Bear country is new for this list

From Lamar Valley on Day 10 through the last Glacier morning on Day 19, this is **grizzly country in a way nothing previously on this hub has been**. Red River Gorge and the Southern Appalachians are black bear country; the food-storage habits transfer, the rest does not.

What changes: **bear spray lives on the person, not in the pack and not in the car.** Noise on blind corners — Iceberg Lake, Grinnell and the Swiftcurrent valley are documented corridors, and the first two are hiked at dawn on this itinerary, which is the highest-encounter window. Everything scented goes in the bear box every night, including toothpaste and the cooler. And in Lamar the rule is simply that you do not leave the pull-out: 25 yards from bison, 100 from bears and wolves, and the valley injures someone walking toward a bison for a photograph roughly every other year.

None of this is a reason not to go. It is a reason to have the habits before Day 10 rather than learning them there.

### What this costs, and what it does not

**The 34-day total isn't added up yet.** The old 21-day loop was about **$1,790**, and unusually for this list that number was nearly complete — **there is no airfare, no rental car and no under-25 surcharge in it**, because you are driving your own car. Compare the [Sky Islands](../sky-islands-2027/) trip: 11 days, ~$1,076, plus an unpriced flight that could add $400 on top.

Fuel is the biggest line at ~$553 and it moves with the route rather than with choices. Campsites are ~$445 across nine places, all estimated. The 25-day version came to $2,038, so **the recut saved about $250** — which was never the argument for shortening it. The argument was day 19.

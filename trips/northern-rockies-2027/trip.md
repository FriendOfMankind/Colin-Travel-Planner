June was checked and it is the gap month on this whole list. Beartooth needs late June at minimum, Snowy Range's campgrounds open July 4, the San Juans hold snow into July. **Moving three weeks fixed more than any destination swap.**

## Notes

### Why this is a July trip, and why that mattered more than the destination

The June road trip was checked destination by destination and June turned out to be the gap month in this entire wishlist. Beartooth needs late June at absolute minimum — US-212 opened May 23 in 2026 and was **closed again by snow until June 9**. Snowy Range's campgrounds open July 4. The San Juans hold snow into early July. Lake Superior is peak blackfly. Utah is too hot, and Colorado's high country is still melting.

**Moving the trip three weeks fixed more than any destination swap did.** Every anchor on this route is comfortably in season in mid-July and none of them was in June. That is the whole reason this page exists in this shape.

### Why Glacier anchors it

✅ **Glacier has no timed-entry vehicle reservation.** It was eliminated for 2026 after five years — across Going-to-the-Sun, Many Glacier, Two Medicine and North Fork — and the registry entry for that park used to be subtitled "worst bureaucracy." That objection is gone. Entry pass only, and the America the Beautiful pass covers it.

What replaced it is smaller and still real: a **3-hour parking cap at Logan Pass from July 1**, and a **$1 ticketed shuttle** instead of the free hop-on service. The shuttle is quietly useful — it is what makes a point-to-point Highline Trail route possible, which is otherwise a car-shuttle problem for a solo traveller.

Camping is where the difficulty moved. 6-month rolling window, released daily, and Many Glacier, Apgar and Avalanche all go the day they open. **Mid-January 2027 is the date that cannot be moved.**

### The 21-day cut: what it bought and what it cost

This started as a 25-day draft and the four days came off the **front end**: Custer State Park and Devils Tower are gone, and Badlands now runs straight into the Bighorns.

That was chosen over cutting Theodore Roosevelt or a Glacier night, and it is the better trade — **it added a seventh Glacier night rather than costing one.** Seven nights is what makes the Glacier block work: an easy Saturday, Iceberg on Sunday, Grinnell on a Monday, the transfer on Tuesday, the Highline Trail on a Wednesday, and a whole day held in reserve on Thursday. The two hardest hikes land on the two best days of the week available.

**Three real costs, none hidden:**
• **Black Elk Peak is gone** — the best hike on the eastern half, and it was nearly free since I-90 passes it either way. Devils Tower goes with it.
• **The altitude step is gone.** The Black Hills at 6,200 ft used to break the climb from the plains to the Bighorns in two. Day 4 is now 2,500 ft to 9,100 ft in one drive, followed by an 11-mile hike on Day 5. The Mirror Lake bail-out exists for exactly that.
• **Fifteen days to the first shower.** Custer State Park was the only mid-route one. Apgar on Day 15 is now it. Cheap to solve with a paid shower in Cody or Great Falls; easy to forget until day nine.

### The length is still the risk, and two days are held against it

🚨 **Nothing on this route is individually hard, and that is exactly why the length is the thing to worry about.**

The profile says **2–5 campgrounds per trip, 5–10 nights**. This is **20 nights across nine places to sleep** — down from 25 nights and eleven places, which is a real improvement, but still roughly double the longest trip on record here, the 11-day [Appalachians](../appalachians-2026/) run.

**Two days are deliberately soft and they are the mitigation:** Day 6 in the Bighorns, after the 11-mile Lost Twin Lakes day, and Day 17 at Glacier, which is genuinely unscheduled with St. Mary Falls as a default rather than a plan. Over three weeks something will be weathered out, closed or simply missed, and those two days are where it goes.

**Theodore Roosevelt was cut on 2026-10-15**, though for a different reason than this lever: the trip now turns west to Oregon for the friends' week instead of driving home on I-94 *(was: "The remaining lever, if 21 still feels long: cut Theodore Roosevelt and drive home in three easier days…")*.

### Two hikes are over the ceiling, on purpose, with bail-outs

The soft ceiling is ~10 mi and ~2,500 ft per day, exceeded "when the payoff justifies it, not by accident." Two scheduled hikes cross it and both are over on distance only:

**Lost Twin Lakes (Day 5): 11.1 mi, 2,076 ft.** A mile over, at 9,000+ ft, on your second day at altitude after the biggest single-day climb of the trip. Bail-out written into the day: Mirror Lake alone at 5.9 mi / 1,095 ft, which is a complete day.

**Grinnell Glacier (Day 14): 11.1 mi, 2,047 ft.** A mile over, on day fourteen. Bail-out: the boat shuttle across Swiftcurrent and Josephine removes about 3.4 miles, and it exists precisely for this.

Three more are named in the Hikes tab and deliberately **not** scheduled: the full Highline Trail (15.2 mi / 2,572 ft — over on both counts, hence the Haystack Butte version), Ptarmigan Tunnel (10.7 mi, now the Day 17 slack option rather than a scheduled day), and Cloud Peak (22.6 mi / 5,337 ft, a backpacking trip and therefore excluded by a locked rule). **Listing what was cut is the point — otherwise the ceiling gets crossed by accident rather than by decision.**

### The Garden Wall ledge, stated plainly

The first mile of the Highline Trail is cut into the side of the Garden Wall with a hand cable bolted to the rock and a long drop below it. It is **not** technical, it is **not** a scramble, and thousands of people walk it every summer including children.

It is also the single highest-consequence piece of ground on this entire route, and the profile is explicit: sustained low-consequence difficulty is welcome, single high-consequence moves get more caution. **So: if the ledge does not feel right, turn around at the cable.** Hidden Lake Overlook from the same parking lot is a genuinely good day and costs nothing but the ego. The page's job here is to give the real numbers and a stated fallback, not to nag and not to stay silent.

### Bear country is new for this list

From Lamar Valley on Day 10 through the last Glacier morning on Day 18, this is **grizzly country in a way nothing previously on this hub has been**. Red River Gorge and the Southern Appalachians are black bear country; the food-storage habits transfer, the rest does not.

What changes: **bear spray lives on the person, not in the pack and not in the car.** Noise on blind corners — Iceberg Lake, Grinnell and the Swiftcurrent valley are documented corridors, and the first two are hiked at dawn on this itinerary, which is the highest-encounter window. Everything scented goes in the bear box every night, including toothpaste and the cooler. And in Lamar the rule is simply that you do not leave the pull-out: 25 yards from bison, 100 from bears and wolves, and the valley injures someone walking toward a bison for a photograph roughly every other year.

None of this is a reason not to go. It is a reason to have the habits before Day 10 rather than learning them there.

### What this costs, and what it does not

**The 33-day total isn't added up yet.** The old 21-day loop was about **$1,790**, and unusually for this list that number was nearly complete — **there is no airfare, no rental car and no under-25 surcharge in it**, because you are driving your own car. Compare the [Sky Islands](../sky-islands-2027/) trip: 11 days, ~$1,076, plus an unpriced flight that could add $400 on top.

Fuel is the biggest line at ~$553 and it moves with the route rather than with choices. Campsites are ~$445 across nine places, all estimated. The 25-day version came to $2,038, so **the recut saved about $250** — which was never the argument for shortening it. The argument was day 19.

### From wishlist/glacier-np.md (updated 2026-09-06)

⚠️ Best hikes per day of anywhere on the list.

⭐ **The subtitle used to say "worst bureaucracy" and that is no longer true.** ✅ **Glacier eliminated the timed-entry vehicle reservation for 2026** — the first time in five years — across Going-to-the-Sun, Many Glacier, Two Medicine and North Fork. You still need an entry pass; you no longer need a slot to drive in. The single largest objection to this park is gone.

⚠️ **What replaced it is smaller but real:** a **3-hour parking limit at Logan Pass from July 1**, and a new **$1 ticketed shuttle** instead of the free hop-on service — a portion of tickets on a 60-day rolling window, the majority released 7 PM the night before. ⚠️ **Camping is still the race:** 6-month rolling window on recreation.gov released daily, and Apgar, Avalanche and Many Glacier go the day they open. A mid-July 2027 trip books around mid-January 2027.

✅ **Confirmed still gone, Sept 2026.** This entry previously carried a re-check on whether 2026's rules would hold; that is closed. The remaining work is entirely the campground race.

⭐ **Scheduled:** seven nights, July 16–22 2027, inside [The Long Way to Glacier](trips/northern-rockies-2027/).

### From wishlist/beartooth-plateau.md (updated 2026-09-06)

📋 US-212 is paved above 9,500 ft, so you get emptiness and short approaches — everywhere else those two trade off. Island Lake CG sits at 9,518 ft. Pairs with Yellowstone's northeast corner for Lamar Valley wildlife with zero hiking.

🛑 **The June 14 2027 target was removed Sept 6 2026, because it contradicted this entry's own window.** "Late June–early/mid September" was already written here and a June 14 start is not late June. The verified detail is worse than the window suggests: ✅ **US-212 opens around Memorial Day but does not stay open** — in 2026 it opened May 23 and was **closed again by snow at the Montana/Wyoming line, reopening June 9**, then ran night closures 7 PM–7 AM Mon–Thu from Pilot Index east to Beartooth Lake. ✅ **Beartooth Lake CG typically opens late June**; sources on Island Lake disagree outright — one says late May, another says **July 1 or earlier depending on snowpack** — and a 9,518 ft campground is exactly where that disagreement is decided by the winter. Snow commonly lingers up high into **early-to-mid July**.

So a mid-June arrival buys a maybe-open road, probably-shut campgrounds and trails under snow. **The plateau is a late-June-at-the-earliest trip and honestly a July one.**

⚠️ **`days` was 21 against `nights: ~7` in the same entry.** The 21 was the road-trip container, not this trip. It is now 12 — seven nights on the plateau plus roughly 2.5 days of driving each way from Avon, which is ~1,700 mi. If a 21-day June road trip happens, this is one anchor inside it, not the whole thing.

⚠️ **The Yellowstone pairing has a lodging problem.** **Pebble Creek** — 27 sites at the foot of the Beartooth Highway and the obvious base for a Lamar Valley dawn — has been **closed for flood recovery** since the June 2022 damage and was still listed closed for 2025; its 2027 status is unknown. The fallback, **Slough Creek, is 16 sites and fills within minutes** of its window opening. Do not assume the Lamar half of this trip has a bed.

### From wishlist/bighorns.md (updated 2026-09-06)

📋 Crazy Woman Canyon Road dispersed camping — creek, canyon walls, the best-reviewed sites in the range. Cut from the overlanding trip and never reallocated.

✅ **Corrected Sept 6 2026 — this was filed as July–September and that was wrong.** Several **Cloud Peak Skyway (US-16) campgrounds open June 5**, which makes the Bighorns the one entry in the alpine tier that is genuinely in season in June. ⚠️ The caveat is elevation-dependent: high-country roads in the range **can stay snow-packed until about July 1**, so the Skyway corridor works in mid-June and the highest side roads may not. That distinction is exactly what a [Beartooth](#beartooth-plateau) trip does not get — US-212 is above 9,500 ft for its whole length, so there is no low half of it to fall back on.

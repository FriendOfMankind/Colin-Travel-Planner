# Sky Islands

> ⚠️ Heart of Rocks and Echo Canyon are the marquee day hikes through rhyolite hoodoo forests — welded ash from the Turkey Creek caldera, so the geology is the whole point rather than a footnote. The Dragoons are the counterpoint at 1.4 billion years, and you can see one range from the other.<br><br>✅ **Verified Sept 2026 — the facts that decide whether March works:** **Chiricahua charges no entrance fee.** **Bonita Canyon (~5,400 ft) is year-round, 25 individual sites + 1 group, all reservable, no walk-ups, 6-month rolling window**, full most nights in its Feb–April peak. **Sunny Flat is year-round first-come — but potable water is off until April**, and **Cochise Stronghold has none at any time of year**: three of eight nights are haul-your-own. 🚨 **Rustler Park and FR 42D are closed Nov–March and unplowed**, so the eight miles between the monument and Portal cost 2h 35m around on I-10, and the high country is off the trip.<br><br>👥 **Rewritten 2026-10-27 for three people over Mar 6–14** *(stated 2026-10-27: two friends, likely yes, both experienced campers and hikers)*. The Bonita nights didn't move. Cut: the Mar 5 arrival day, Wasson Peak, and one of the two Cave Creek days.<br><br>**March is the monument's busiest month, and that's fine.** The hoodoo hikes are the reason to come. Timing beats the crowd: Heart of Rocks, the biggest day, lands on a Wednesday at dawn. The split (**3 nights in the monument, 5 outside it**) gives the trip its empty places, Cave Creek and the Dragoons, which is what the crowd rule asks for (`me/travel-style.md`).

## Overview

A clockwise loop out of Tucson: Saguaro West → Cochise Stronghold (Dragoons) → Chiricahua National Monument from the Willcox side → **north around the range via I-10** → Cave Creek Canyon at Portal → back to Tucson through Douglas and Bisbee. **No road is driven twice.** The one road that would shorten it — FR 42 over Onion Saddle, straight through the Chiricahuas — is the road you cannot use. See the warnings.

### The crowd problem

March is Chiricahua's peak month. The crowd rule says the icon is worth its crowd, provided the trip also has somewhere empty (`me/travel-style.md`). **3 nights in the monument, 5 outside it** (Gilbert Ray, Cochise Stronghold, Cave Creek). Cave Creek and the Dragoons are the same geology with a fraction of the people.

### No entrance fee

✅ Chiricahua National Monument charges **no entrance fee** — it was eliminated when the campground moved to reservations. The America the Beautiful pass does nothing here. It is not needed.

### Time zone

Arizona does **not** observe daylight saving. **DST starts Sun Mar 14 2027, the day you fly home.** The clock at camp doesn't move, but the flight home and everyone at home shift an hour relative to you. Check the departure in Arizona local time the night before.

### Water

**Two of the four campgrounds have no drinking water in March.** Cochise Stronghold has none ever; Sunny Flat's is off until April. That's 3 of 8 nights haul-your-own, for three people.

### Crowds: the icon plus the empty places

**March is Chiricahua National Monument's single busiest month**, and the campground is full most nights. Under the crowd rule (`me/travel-style.md`), that's no reason to skip the hoodoos. The question is whether the trip also has empty places, and it does: **three nights in the monument, five outside it.** The Dragoons and Cave Creek Canyon are the same sky-island geology (Cave Creek's walls are the same welded tuff as the hoodoos) with a fraction of the visitors. Heart of Rocks, the biggest day, is on a Wednesday to beat the crowd.

### Water is the actual risk here, not cold

**Three of eight nights are at campgrounds with no potable water** — Cochise Stronghold has none at any time of year, and Sunny Flat's is shut off until April, which is exactly the fact that makes an early-March trip different from the April version of the same trip. With three people, that's a lot of jugs. Add the Heart of Rocks loop, which has no water on it. The mitigation is dull and it works: maximum carrying capacity in the car, fill at every town, and treat the filter as a backstop rather than a plan.

### The road you cannot take

On a map, Chiricahua National Monument and Portal are about eight miles apart across the range, connected by FR 42 over Onion Saddle. In March that road is **unpaved, unplowed and closed** — it is the same road that serves Rustler Park, which shuts with the first snow and stays shut through March. The crossing that exists is I-10: north out of Bowie, east to San Simon, then south into Portal. **2 hours 35 minutes to travel eight miles of mountain.**

### The geology is the through-line, and it is one event

Roughly 27 million years ago the Turkey Creek caldera erupted and laid down a sheet of rhyolite ash so hot it welded into solid rock as it settled. Cooling cracked it into vertical columns; water and ice have been widening those joints ever since. **Everything you walk on Days 4 and 5 is one eruption, weathered.** The hoodoos at Heart of Rocks, the corridor at Echo Canyon, and the canyon walls above Sunny Flat are the same rock cut three different ways.

The Dragoons are the counterpoint and they are older by two orders of magnitude — around 1.4-billion-year-old granite, exhumed and weathered into domes. Two days apart, a billion and a half years of separation, and you can see one range from the other.

### Ruins, honestly

`me/principles.md` 10 (sharpened 2026-10-27): **only truly abandoned ruins count**; stabilized, interpretive ones are drive-bys. By that test: **Slavin's Mine** (timbers and tailings at the head of a canyon, almost nobody walks it) is the real one, and the day to protect. **Fort Bowie** is adobe stubs inside a National Historic Site, likely the polished kind; not researched. **Bisbee** and **Tombstone** are towns, not ruins. **Gould Mine** went with Wasson Peak.

Cochise Stronghold is the odd one: a ruin with no structures. It is the natural fortress the Chiricahua Apache held for a decade, and Cochise is buried somewhere in it in a location that was deliberately never recorded.

### The new moon, and why nothing is built on it

New moon falls on March 7–8 2027, mid-trip, in one of the darkest corners of the lower 48. That is a real fact and it is in the schedule because that is where sun and moon lines go.

**It is not scheduled as an activity, on purpose.** Night-sky watching is on the hub's declined list. With friends along it may be worth asking again; if the answer changes, the trip has an obvious free upgrade sitting in it.
